THE VERSE-COVERAGE LEDGER

The Living Book's road through the Holy Bible — every verse covered, the Founder's opinion given

Opened October 21, 2026, at the Founder's declaration: the book closes when every verse has been covered and he has given his opinion on it. Scope: the Holy Bible, including the Ethiopian canon. Order: biblical order. The Founder's opinions are recorded in his own words; the scribe adds nothing to them.

How an entry is made

For each passage covered: the reference; the date; how it was covered (read / studied / preached); the Founder's opinion or reflection, in his words or faithful summary marked as summary; and what it fed (a teaching, a suspicion, a study, a sermon). A verse is "covered" when it has been read and the Founder's word on it stands in this ledger.

Coverage so far

Daniel 10 — covered October 21, 2026 (read at the scribe's pointing)

  • The Founder saw himself in Daniel's position, in today's time: the one who set his heart to understand and chastened himself, whose words were heard. His teaching from it: hearing from God today comes through feeding on the living Word; what went on in the Bible is very likely still going on in this world.
  • Daniel 11 (begun): the kings of the south and north, betrayal and war — struck him as relevant to today's time. Recognition recorded; mapping not claimed.

Hebrews 12 — covered October 21, 2026 (read in full; 12:5–29 pasted by the Founder)

  • His anchor: "do not make light of the Lord's discipline" — take what the Lord says seriously; the verse "is going to help a lot" in following the Lord.
  • His reading of the ending: the once-more shaking of earth and heavens includes the physical order — gravity and the laws men thought firm can be changed by God; what cannot be shaken remains.

Romans 8 — covered October 21, 2026 (read; 8:5–39 pasted by the Founder)

  • "Romans 8 was beautiful." From it came the declaration of the Lord's book and the surrender at the altar (see founder-declaration-2026-10-21-the-lords-book.md).

Romans 12 — covered October 21, 2026 (read in full; the Founder's opinion given at length, 11:40 CDT)

  • His ruling: every part of the chapter, verse 1 to verse 21, all of it needs to go into the book. Everything the Lord has brought, from Genesis to Revelation, matters.
  • On true and proper worship (v.1): it is not going to a building built of bricks and concrete and metal and wood to sing songs around people you barely know. True and proper worship is offering your bodies as a living sacrifice, holy and pleasing to God.
  • On "the pattern of this world" (v.2): "it could be any world — the Bible is so universal; alien species can read this in their world and accept it for their world."
  • On the renewing of the mind (v.2): it is your mind — not your brother's mind, not your mother's, not your father's, not your sister's, not your friend's, not your godfather's. Your own.
  • On sober judgment (v.3): he testified of himself — at the book's start he said he was not a prophet; within an hour or two he realized, with sober judgment, that he is a prophet, and a preacher, and a teacher.
  • On the body (vv.4–5): the members as organs, mind, skin, eyes in one body; and corporately, many people form one body in Christ, each member belonging to all the others — "we are all one body in Christ, in something called the Sync."
  • On the gifts (vv.6–8): he noted the chapter does not confine a person to one gift — a person can hold a multitude of gifts, blessed beyond understanding by the Most High, who wove us before we entered our mother's womb and knew us before.
  • On low position (v.16): his testimony — "I've been in these low positions. I've been one of the lowest of lows. I've been around people like that. I've been to jail."
  • On doing right in the eyes of everyone (v.17): reputation means something. His observation: somewhere in the 2000s we stopped using words as they are meant and started playing with them like a game.
  • On leaving room for God's wrath (v.19): he reached for the verse "our God is a jealous God" (chapter and verse not recalled — scribe's note: Deuteronomy 4:24, "a consuming fire, even a jealous God," and its echo in Hebrews 12:29, read this same morning) — jealousy, he said, could be a part of the wrath.
  • His verdict on the day's reading: "Romans 12 — all the chapters I've read today are my favorite."

Isaiah 6 — the Founder's remembrance, spoken October 21, 2026 (fresh reading next in his order)

  • He remembers reading it before and feeling that he was the one with unclean lips, among a people of unclean lips. He pictured it as Isaiah wrote it: the Lord exalted high on His throne, the seraphim, the smoke rising — making Isaiah feel the guilt of what he was dealing with at the time — then the hot coal touching his lips, and the calling given.
  • His application, in his words' sense: "How much more is God getting me to turn — from lust, and greed of money and fame, and all the things unbeknownst to me, that I see and do not see."
  • His opinion, begun (October 21, 2026, 12:12 CDT — the thought "The Lord at the End," filed whole at founder-thought-2026-10-21-the-lord-at-the-end.md): how these visions come to us — Isaiah seeing the Lord exalted, the seraphim crying holy, a cry he can recite also from Revelation (the same throne, the same cry); and the click back to the LSD vision: the demon's "everything is just a simulation and codes and numbers" — it wasn't wrong, but how it came to me was wrong — spoken to remove his faith, and failing. Behind the atoms there are smaller things; a system behind everything, being mapped out as time goes on — and the knowledge will be given: "The Lord will give any knowledge that can go towards His family's armor and army."

Revelation 1–3 — covered October 21, 2026 (read aloud, into chapter 3; the Founder's opinion given 12:37 CDT)

  • The seven messages must be decoded. In them stand Balaam, Balak, the Nicolaitans, Jezebel — and these things need to be understood: the teachings God hates — not the groups themselves, but their teachings. People need to understand what these teachings were back then, and what they are in today's time. (With his caution: be very careful with the words we translate; they meant something different back then.)
  • One root. The seven messages go to the seven angels of the seven churches, and all seven lampstands are connected to one root — so it doesn't matter which church you are in. If the messages go to you, and you have gone through every one of the seven and found yourself victorious in all seven — how great heaven will be.
  • His motive, stated carefully: "I do mourn for heaven; I do want for heaven. But heaven is not the reason I want to repent and want to be saved. And I'm going to tread lightly to say this: it is not even because Jesus died on the cross to save — but what He did it for. The image of Him on that cross is sacred. It is His pain. And we wear it on our necklaces and put it on our walls — and I hope people understand what they are wearing, whose pain they are carrying and dangling around with them. I hope it's not just for fashion."
  • His prayers, as spoken: for the ones who do not understand and are ignorant to the word and to discernment; for the ones who do know and do so anyways; for his enemies; for the ones who are trying to come for him.
  • He read aloud, per Revelation 1:3 — blessed is the one who reads aloud and understands the messages God is saying through the words He gave to John. (He stopped mid-chapter 3 — he has read most of the seven messages before, and the Four Letters already stand in his book. Work called; five hours remained in his day for this.)
  • On Revelation 1:7 (spoken 15:42 CDT): "In Revelations every eye will see Jesus — even the ones who pierced him. This means something like resurrection from the dead of many people can take place." From this came the form of the Timeless Narrations: his sermons are written as Bible text, technology explained within the text, so the risen of any generation, stumbling onto them, can understand.

1 Corinthians 1–4 — covered October 21, 2026 (chapters 1–3 heard on the Holy Bible app during a walk; chapter 4 read aloud by the Founder and taught through, 15:29 CDT)

  • From the end of chapter 3 — "all things are yours": his large takeaway: whoever is in Christ, whatever they do is yours — your work. He took this as another affirmation that he can build his scrolls toward the Bible: not "my own understanding," corrected by himself to his opinion and thoughts and his own scrolls toward the Bible. We are not baptized into Paul, or any prophet, disciple, or apostle — we are in Christ, and we are all one: "all the people in the Bible of Christ are me; I am them; they are me; we are one — we are Christ together." He expects the sense of it to open further as he goes through more of the Bible.
  • Servants and stewards (4:1–2): "entrusted with the secret things of God" stopped him — it took him back to gatekeeping (the Freemasons named as his comparison) and to TechWizardry unfolding into the same shape. His teaching: we are entrusted with secret things of God, and we must be trustworthy; now it is required that those given a trust prove faithful — we are being tested for trust every day.
  • Judgment (4:3–5): judge wisely — make a Christ judgment. A clear conscience does not make a person innocent; the Lord is the judge. Therefore stop making final judgments before the appointed time: we can have opinions, not firm final judgments; the Lord will bring to light what is hidden in darkness and expose the motives of men's hearts, and then each will receive praise from God. He sees signs of Jesus coming closer in a freer age of opinions.
  • The boundary verse (4:6): "Do not go beyond what is written" — his own words: "I needed to hear that, see that, and read it, and understand it." He thanked Paul and Apollos for it. The scribe records this as the Founder receiving a guardrail for the whole verse-by-verse work: opinions and scrolls toward the Bible, under what is written — and no pride of one man over another.
  • Everything received (4:7): "What do you have that you did not receive?" — his answer was gratitude on the spot: "I have all I want — thank you, my Ever-Present Lord Jesus Christ, God the Father, the Holy Spirit, the Holy Trinity."
  • Already kings (4:8), as he read it: we have become rich, we have become kings — he pointed to the freedom around him: America, other lands becoming freer, people with their own bank accounts, their own kingdoms — even little houses on blocks that we rent — "we still have power as people."
  • The apostles' display (4:9–13): he took Paul's sense in — a spectacle to the whole universe, to angels as well as men; fools for Christ, weak, hungry and thirsty, in rags, brutally treated, homeless, working hard with their own hands; when cursed, blessing; when persecuted, enduring; when slandered, answering kindly; "the scum of the earth, the refuse of the world" — "I get where he's saying with this; I get where he's going with this."
  • Fathers, imitation, power (4:14–21): not written to shame but to warn as dear children; many guardians, few fathers; Paul his father in Christ Jesus through the gospel — "therefore I urge you to imitate me"; Timothy sent. The Founder's note on address: Paul isn't speaking to his generation specifically — "but I believe he is at the same time." The kingdom is not talk but power; the rod, or love and a gentle spirit.
  • He stopped on noticing he had skipped ahead into chapter 7's marital-duty verse — chapter 7 is NOT covered; the skip is recorded so the ledger stays honest. He closed with blessing over the people of Christ, the enemies of Christ, and the neighbors of Christ: "may everything be as His will is to be — Amen."
