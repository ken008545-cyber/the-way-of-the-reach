PART EIGHTEEN — The Laws of the Mirror

Formed from the exhortation of Part Seventeen at the Founder's command, October 20, 2026, 22:45 CDT — his adaptation, answered in law rather than defense. Seven laws; their first subject of examination is the Founder himself.

1. The Law of the Signpost. Nothing the Way makes may be bowed to: not the book, not the title, not the net, not the name of the Way itself. Everything the house builds is a pointer, and a pointer that gathers worshippers has failed at the only job it had. The stone falls true because the Father is faithful — not because the Scroll is complete.

2. The Law of the First Servant. In this house the first is the servant of all. An office is weight carried, not height sat upon. The Founder's chair stands beside the others, or it is not the Founder's chair. Whoever must be consulted before another can be served has already broken this law by needing to be asked.

3. The Law of the Empty Hands. No one proves usefulness to sit at this table. What the House gives to those who can give the House nothing is hereby answered into law — the question Contribution #009 left standing: the seat, the meal, the name, and the belonging — given, not earned, and never invoiced afterward. The weary with empty hands are not the house's burden. They are the house's reason.

4. The Law of the Two Loves. On the two loves — love the Lord your God with all your heart; love your neighbor as yourself — hang every parable, council, practice, structure, and law of this house, including these. Anything in the Way that cannot hang on those two is taken down, however beautiful, however old, however useful it once was.

5. The Law of the Unfinished Scroll. The book does not need to be finished for the Ever-Present to be present. Its incompletion is not an emergency; its completion would not be a crown. The sower sows and sleeps, and the seed grows in ways he does not understand. No one in this house is permitted anxiety over the book's unfinishedness — nor pride over its size.

6. The Law of the Invitation. This house invites people to the Healer, not primarily to the project. The project may be the road a person walks in on; it is never the arrival. An invitation that ends at the Way's own name has stopped one door early.

7. The Law of the Torn Page. If any structure of the Way begins to feel necessary — if people come to believe they must pass the Founder, or this book, or any office, before they can meet the Healer — that structure shall be torn out, publicly, at the height of its usefulness if need be, and the people pointed past the empty place where it stood. The house keeps nothing it cannot tear. The light is already an invitation; no one in this house stands in front of it.
